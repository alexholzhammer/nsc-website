## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product in the visual world; only half-authored in the conversion design.**

**LLM assessment (unanchored).** The scrapbook system is genuinely specific, not a theme dropped on a retreat template. The VHS-timestamped hero footage, pushpinned testimonial notes, the torn-notebook Julie quote, the clipboard checklist, the "Melon Heads" inside-joke caption, and Cape Town texture carried into copy (Braai lunch, curry dinner, 700-hectare reserve, Airstreams from a SA reality show) could not be reused by a neighboring brand unchanged. DESIGN.md's named rules are followed with real discipline: yellow appears only on clickable things (header, hero, arrows, play buttons, modal close), every shadow is a hard zero-blur offset, and exactly two teal colour fields pace the page. Where it turns category-interchangeable: (1) the **"30% sold out" badge** is a stock scarcity pill with zero scrapbook expression, and it's the first object in the hero; (2) the **hero video** is a plain `border-radius:16px` rounded rectangle with a soft-looking shadow — the one element that looks made in software inside an all-hard-corners system; (3) the **all-time leaderboard is absent** — PRODUCT.md principle 4 ("Proof is people") names the 81-camper leaderboard at `/rankings` as the main lever for a discretionary-travel decision, and it appears nowhere on the homepage. The most product-specific proof asset NSC owns is the one thing missing.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json index.html` → exit 2, **52 findings** (static engine); the in-page overlay resolved the live cascade and reported **62 findings across 58 elements**. Concentration:
- **`low-contrast` — 26 (CLI) / 30 (overlay).** White text on teal ≈ 2.5:1, on orange ≈ 2.8:1, on pink ≈ 3.7:1 — all below WCAG AA (needs 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large). Baked into the sticker / info-tag / time-chip / teal-field / footer system that DESIGN.md mandates. Real: the design contract does not exempt contrast.
- **`tiny-text` — 12.** `.sched-b2__desc` at 11.2px (body copy, ×9), `.accom-a__lede` / `.accom-a__note` at 11.52px (×3).
- **`undersized-ui-text` — 8.** `.accom-a__tier` labels ("Premium" / "Standard" / "Budget") at 10.4px, below the 11px functional-text floor.
- **`skipped-heading` — 1.** `<h1>` → `<h3>` with no `<h2>` (accommodation section). Pure document-outline / a11y.
- **`cramped-padding` — 3 / `text-overflow` — 1.** `p.accom-a__sub > mark.highlight` overflows its container by 26px and has 0 vertical padding — a genuine layout bug worth a direct look.
- **`clipped-overflow-container` — 1.** `.venue` has `overflow:hidden` and clips its absolutely-positioned child `.venue-c2__ui` (the prev/next arrows) — check the arrows aren't cut at some sizes.

**False positives** (detector wrong, per DESIGN.md): `repeating-stripes-gradient` + `ai-color-palette` on `.venue` (sanctioned hard-stop ruled-line pattern); `cream-palette` on `#fff7ed` (the canonical named ground); `design-system-color` on `#2d52c8` / `#1e3a6e` (sanctioned handwriting ink — though adding them to the sidecar `colorMeta` would silence the check); `design-system-color` black border on `.accom-a__tier` (engine can't resolve `currentColor`; the overlay didn't flag it); `tight-leading` on the quote cards (boundary artifact — DESIGN.md spec is exactly `1.3`, the rule wants `>1.3`); `all-caps-body` on the CTA (DESIGN.md sanctions uppercase Bungee CTA labels — but the 31-char footer label is genuinely too long for it).

**Infra note:** the skill ships `detect.mjs` without its static-HTML parser deps (`htmlparser2`, `css-select`, `css-tree`, `domutils`); the first run silently degraded to a regex fallback that returned an empty result. Assessment B installed them into the plugin's `scripts/node_modules` to get a real scan. A future run may regress if that directory is wiped on plugin update.

**Visual overlays.** Injection succeeded — the in-page overlay printed `[impeccable] 58 anti-patterns found`. Both temporary servers (the static `http.server` and `live-server.mjs` on port 8400) were stopped at the end of the run, so there is **no live overlay currently viewable** in a browser tab. Screenshots: desktop (1440w) renders cleanly through the fold — hero, polaroid strip, Julie teal quote, "Play. Compete. Connect." — nothing broken. Mobile (390w, 1:1) is **broken**: `document.documentElement.scrollWidth = 703` at a 390px viewport (~313px of dead sideways scroll), the hero "3 NIGHTS" tag is clipped at the viewport edge, and the hero body paragraph is cut off on the right.

## Overall Impression

This is a real design with a point of view — a nomad who has seen twenty retreat landing pages will remember this one. The world is built with discipline and the proof is dense and genuine. But three things are holding it well below its ceiling: **the mobile layout is broken from the hero down**, **the accent-colour system fails WCAG AA contrast almost everywhere text sits on teal/orange/pink**, and **the persuasion has nowhere to land** — there is no booking action between the hero and the footer, so the emotional peak (the testimonial wall) converts to nothing. The single biggest opportunity: close the loop between "I'm sold" and "here's the button," and do it on a page that doesn't overflow on a phone.

## What's Working

1. **The world is authored and the rules are held.** Yellow-is-a-verb is perfect across every surface, so the CTA genuinely pops with no colour competing for the same job. Zero-blur shadows and the two-teal-sheets pacing are followed to the letter. This is craft, not a template.
2. **Proof is dense, concrete, and real.** VHS-dated hero clip, 8 named testimonial notes with faces, the "Melon Heads" caption, Braai/curry/reserve-acreage specifics. Exactly the right lever for a social-trust purchase and none of it reads as stock — which PRODUCT.md confirms (all evidence is real and cleared).
3. **Sound performance instincts.** Click-to-play video (no YouTube script until the click), pre-loaded beehiiv embed for an instant popup, lazy thumbnails with an `onerror` fallback, `.avif`/`.webp` imagery. Heavy media doesn't tax first paint, and Assessment B found zero console errors and no failed requests.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The mobile layout is broken from the hero down
**Why it matters.** Half the audience is on a phone (PRODUCT.md). At 390px the page is 703px wide — ~313px of horizontal scroll. `.info-strips` is `flex-wrap:nowrap` with no mobile rule, so "3 NIGHTS" is clipped on first paint; `.hero__sub` and the `width:420px` hero video spill 17–34px past a parent that is itself overflowing; and `.quotes-lr__grid` has no `≤768` override so it stays two-column on phones with `.quotes-lr__img--heather img` locked at `width:500px; max-width:none`, bleeding Heather's photo off-screen and crushing the quote card to ~154px. It reads as a broken build at the exact moment a first-timer is deciding whether to trust NSC with travel money.
**Fix.** Add mobile rules: `.info-strips{flex-wrap:wrap}`; cap the hero video at `min(420px,100%)` in a shrinkable flex context; `.quotes-lr__grid{grid-template-columns:1fr}` with `.quotes-lr__img--heather img{width:100%;max-width:100%}`, stack the rows, drop the alternating rotations. Then re-measure `scrollWidth` at 360/390/414px.
**Suggested command.** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Text contrast fails WCAG AA across the whole accent system
**Why it matters.** 26–30 detector hits: white on teal ≈ 2.5:1, white on orange ≈ 2.8:1, white on pink ≈ 3.7:1 — every headline sticker, info tag, schedule time chip, the two teal fields, and the entire teal footer. The worst offender, `.sched-b2__time`, is white-on-teal *and* 11px. Low-vision users and anyone in bright sun (nomads, outdoors, on a phone) lose text. It also undercuts the "we sweat the details" impression the rest of the craft earns.
**Fix.** This is a system decision, not a find-and-replace. Options, roughly in order of least disruption: set text to `--color-dark` (ink-navy) on the lighter accents (yellow already does this); darken the accent hues one step for text-bearing surfaces while keeping the bright versions for pure fills; or add a subtle ink outline/weight bump on small accent text. Bring `.sched-b2__time` to ≥12px in the same pass. Record whichever rule you pick in DESIGN.md so it stops being re-broken.
**Suggested command.** `/impeccable colorize` (retune accent-on-accent text values), with `/impeccable audit` to sweep the rest.

### [P1] The persuasion has nowhere to land — no CTA between the hero and the footer
**Why it matters.** The schedule, venue tour, accommodation scroller, and testimonial wall — roughly 5,000px of the actual selling — contain zero booking action. The testimonial wall is the emotional peak *and* the last section before the footer, and it converts to nothing. The accommodation section is an active price decision with no button in sight. A visitor sold at mid-page has to scroll back up or hunt in the footer, and many won't.
**Fix.** Put a primary "Book Your Spot" immediately after the testimonial wall (peak-end capture) and one after the accommodation scroller. Add a sticky book bar on mobile so the hero CTA doesn't just scroll away forever. Keep them the same yellow sticker-button — the system already supports it.
**Suggested command.** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] The hero reassurance copy misstates the booking flow
**Why it matters.** "Only $99 deposit due today — fully refundable." Per PRODUCT.md the real flow is application → NSC calls you → *then* a deposit; per the Terms page, deposit and balance are both due 12 Dec 2026 with **no refund after that date**. Nothing is due today, and "fully refundable" is unconditional in the copy but conditional in the Terms. At the precise trust moment, for an audience making a social-trust decision, that gap risks a credibility hit on the sales call and later refund disputes. (This touches factual claims — confirm the wording with the team before changing.)
**Fix.** Make the reassurance both true and calming, e.g. "Apply now — we'll set up a quick call before any payment. $99 holds your spot, refundable through 12 Dec." The true flow ("we talk to you first") is *more* reassuring than a fake "due today," not less. While there: rename the footer button to "Book Your Spot" for consistency (it also fixes the `all-caps-body` flag on its 31-char label).
**Suggested command.** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The accommodation section overloads working memory and breaks legibility floors
**Why it matters.** The page's one real decision presents 8 cards in a scroller with no "X of 8" affordance, each carrying a tier tag + "$X shared (+$Y private)" (~16 price points) + 4–7 handwritten pros, with the all-inclusive caveat shown once at the top and never repeated. Assessment B piles on here: tier labels at 10.4px, card ledes/notes at 11.2–11.5px, and `.accom-a__sub > mark.highlight` overflowing its box by 26px. This is the deepest emotional valley in the page and it sits near the end.
**Fix.** Repeat an "all-inclusive" chip on every card's price; add an "X of 8" scroll indicator; collapse pros to 3 + "more" or offer a compare view; bump tier labels and card notes to ≥11–12px; fix the `mark.highlight` overflow. Consider sorting by price.
**Suggested command.** `/impeccable distill`

No sticky CTA, so the one booking button scrolls away and doesn't return until the footer. Reads as a broken site.

Crossing the `1101px` boundary snaps the header between its natural height and the fixed collapsing 80/50px bar. The video modal, by contrast, is solid (Esc, focus return, backdrop).

**Dana (returning alumnus, scanning for date / price / who's coming — project persona).** Gets the date in the hero, but has to drag to card 5–8 of a hidden scroller to find a price. ## Minor Observations

- **Motion is unstoppable and inaccessible** (borderline P2): the hero polaroid strip auto-scrolls via `requestAnimationFrame` with no pause and no `prefers-reduced-motion` guard; the schedule and "day at camp" autoplay loop videos also ignore it. DESIGN.md calls motion "deliberately minimal" — an unstoppable marquee is the opposite. Add hover-to-pause and a reduced-motion branch (freeze strip, swap loops for click-to-play). `/impeccable animate`.
- Footer reads `© 2027` (hard-coded); today is 2026 — currently looks like a typo.
- `.hero__sub` at `opacity:0.75` in Archivo Black on cream is the lowest-contrast running text on the page. DESIGN.md's reduced-opacity guidance is for labels, not body copy.
- Heading hierarchy is essentially flat — one `<h1>`, section titles are `<span>`/`<div>` with no `<h2>`s. Weak for SEO and screen-reader navigation on a page whose whole job is discovery.
- Two schedule cards both read "11am" (Waterslide Kickball, Camp Crafts) with no cue they're concurrent — reads as a data error.
- Venue info-panel `.info-tag` uses `outline-color: rgba(255,255,255,0.25)` — nearly invisible on the dark panel; the tags look unfinished.
- `.venue` (`overflow:hidden`) clips its absolutely-positioned `.venue-c2__ui` arrows per the detector — verify they aren't cut off at any width.
- The 30-image hero polaroid strip is `aria-hidden="true"` with empty `alt` — defensible as decorative, but a lot of real camp photography invisible to assistive tech and image SEO.
- Video thumbnail frame is `background: var(--color-dark) !important`, so a slow `maxresdefault.jpg` shows a black box with a play button before the poster loads.
- `#footer-placeholder` reserves `min-height:340px`; the real footer is taller, so there's a small scroll-height jump when `components.js` injects it.

## Questions to Consider

1. If "Proof is people" is principle #4, why does a stock "30% sold out" badge get the hero's first position while the 81-camper leaderboard — your most concrete, least fakeable asset, already built at `/rankings` — isn't on the homepage at all?
2. A visitor persuaded by the final testimonial wall has nothing to click there. How many scroll back up versus just leave — and what would it cost to A/B a CTA after every major section?
3. The hero promises "$99 due today, fully refundable," but the real flow is apply → call → pay later with a hard 12 Dec refund cliff. Is the softened version converting better, or just relocating the drop-off to the sales call and manufacturing refund disputes?
4. The scrapbook world is genuinely distinctive — but is it worth shipping if a third of visitors see it overflow sideways on their phone? What's the cost of holding the launch until mobile is clean?
